Exam Guidelines • CAPS Paper 2 Topics

This is a simplified study outline of everything Mpumi must know for Chemistry Paper 2. Each topic now includes worked examples with simple, step‑by‑step memos, plus links to the matching practice questions.

1. Representing Chemical Change

Core: write and balance equations; conserve atoms and mass; use state symbols.

Tips: Balance metals → non‑metals → O and H last. Use coefficients, not subscripts. Check total charge if ionic.

Worked Example 1 (Balancing)
Balance: C3H8 + O2 → CO2 + H2O
Memo (simple): 3 C → 3 CO₂; 8 H → 4 H₂O; O on RHS = 3×2 + 4×1 = 10 O → need 5 O₂. Final: C₃H₈ + 5O₂ → 3CO₂ + 4H₂O.
Worked Example 2 (States & Ions)
Write states: neutralisation of strong acid and base.
Memo: HCl(aq) + NaOH(aq) → NaCl(aq) + H₂O(ℓ). Net ionic: H⁺ + OH⁻ → H₂O.

2. Quantitative Aspects of Chemical Change
  • Moles: n = m/M; gases at STP: 22.4 dm³·mol⁻¹.
  • Solutions: C = n/V (mol·dm⁻³).
  • Stoichiometry: limiting reagent, % yield, % purity.
Worked Example 1 (Limiting reagent)
Given 10.0 g Mg reacts with 0.50 mol HCl in Mg + 2HCl → MgCl₂ + H₂. Find limiting reagent and moles H₂.
Memo: n(Mg)=10.0/24.3≈0.41 mol; need 0.82 mol HCl but only 0.50 mol available → HCl limits. Ratio 2:1 gives n(H₂)=0.50/2=0.25 mol.
Worked Example 2 (Concentration)
How many moles in 250.0 cm³ of 0.200 M Na₂SO₄?
Memo: V=0.2500 dm³; n=CV=0.200×0.2500=0.0500 mol.
Try Q5 & stoich in Q2
3. Intermolecular Forces

London (all), Dipole–dipole (polar), H‑bonding (H with N/O/F). Stronger IMF → higher boiling point, melting point; lower vapour pressure.

Worked Example 1 (Ranking BP)
Order by increasing BP: propane, propanone, propan‑1‑ol.
Memo: propane (London) < propanone (dipole) < propan‑1‑ol (H‑bonding).
Worked Example 2 (Branching)
Which has lower BP: pentane or 2‑methylbutane? Why?
Memo: 2‑methylbutane (more branching → smaller surface area → weaker London forces).

5. Organic Reactions

Addition (H₂, HBr, H₂O), substitution, elimination, esterification (conc. H₂SO₄, heat, reflux).

Worked Example 1 (Markovnikov)
Predict major product: propene + HBr (no peroxide).
Memo: H adds to C with more H → 2‑bromopropane.
Worked Example 2 (Esterification)
Write equation: ethanol + ethanoic acid → ? Give conditions.
Memo: CH₃CH₂OH + CH₃COOH ⇌ CH₃COOCH₂CH₃ + H₂O; conc. H₂SO₄, heat, reflux.
Go to Q4 practice
6. Energy and Heats of Reaction

ΔH sign, exo vs endo, activation energy, energy profiles, bond‑enthalpy estimates: ΔH ≈ ΣE(broken) − ΣE(formed).

Worked Example 1 (Bond energies)
Estimate ΔH for H₂ + ½O₂ → H₂O(g). Data: H–H 436; O=O 498; O–H 463 (kJ·mol⁻¹).
Memo: break 436 + 0.5×498 = 685; form 2×463 = 926; ΔH ≈ 685 − 926 = −241 kJ·mol⁻¹ (exo).
Worked Example 2 (Profile)
Sketch exothermic profile and label Ea(fwd), Ea(rev), ΔH.
Memo: products below reactants; ΔH is vertical drop; Ea arrows from levels to peak.

7. Rates of Reaction

Rate = Δc/Δt; factors: nature, surface area, concentration/pressure, temperature, catalyst; collision theory; MB curves.

Worked Example 1 (Collision theory)
Explain why higher temperature increases rate.
Memo: particles move faster; more have E ≥ Ea → more effective collisions per second.
Worked Example 2 (Catalyst)
What changes on the Maxwell–Boltzmann curve when a catalyst is used?
Memo: Ea threshold effectively lowers (shift left) → larger fraction above Ea; peak shape similar.
Go to Q6 practice
8. Chemical Equilibrium

Closed systems, reversible reactions, dynamic equilibrium, Le Châtelier; write Kc; do ICE tables; interpret graphs.

Worked Example 1 (Kc value)
For N₂ + 3H₂ ⇌ 2NH₃, write Kc and say what a small Kc means.
Memo: Kc = [NH₃]²/([N₂][H₂]³); small Kc → mostly reactants at equilibrium.
Worked Example 2 (Le Châtelier)
Effect of increasing pressure on the Haber process.
Memo: fewer moles on RHS (2 vs 4) → higher pressure shifts right → more NH₃.
Go to Q7 practice
9. Acids and Bases

Brønsted–Lowry, conjugate pairs, strong vs weak, pH and Ka/Kb, hydrolysis, titration logic and indicators.

Worked Example 1 (Titration)
25.00 cm³ of 0.100 M HCl is titrated with 0.125 M NaOH. Find V at equivalence and pH there.
Memo: n(HCl)=0.02500×0.100=0.002500 mol; need same moles NaOH → V=0.002500/0.125=0.02000 L=20.00 cm³; strong–strong → pH ≈ 7 at 25 °C.
Worked Example 2 (Hydrolysis)
Predict pH effect: CH₃COONa(aq) vs NH₄Cl(aq).
Memo: acetate salt basic (OH⁻ formed) → pH>7; ammonium salt acidic (H₃O⁺ formed) → pH<7.
Go to Q8 practice
10. Electrochemistry

Galvanic vs electrolytic, anode/cathode signs, half‑reactions, E°cell = E°cath − E°an, industrial electrolysis.

Worked Example 1 (Cell notation & half‑reactions)
For Zn|Zn²⁺(1.0 M)||Cu²⁺(1.0 M)|Cu, identify anode/cathode and write both half‑reactions.
Memo: anode: Zn → Zn²⁺ + 2e⁻; cathode: Cu²⁺ + 2e⁻ → Cu; electrons: Zn → Cu.
Worked Example 2 (E°cell)
Use E°(Zn²⁺/Zn)=−0.76 V; E°(Cu²⁺/Cu)=+0.34 V to find E°cell.
Memo: E°cell = 0.34 − (−0.76) = +1.10 V → spontaneous.
